CORROSION

Concept Analysis

🧠 UNDERSTAND THE CONCEPT

Corrosion Penetration Rate

Understand the corrosion science behind CPR, how corrosion occurs, what affects its rate, and how CPR is used in engineering.

Corrosion is the gradual deterioration of a metal due to chemical or electrochemical interaction with its surrounding environment.

Key Idea

Corrosion causes a metal to lose material over time, reducing its thickness and potentially affecting its strength and life.

Electrochemical corrosion occurs when anodic and cathodic reactions take place on a metal surface in the presence of an electrolyte.

Anodic Reaction

M → Mⁿ⁺ + ne⁻

Metal atoms lose electrons and enter the surrounding electrolyte as ions.

Cathodic Reaction

The electrons released at the anodic region are consumed by a reduction reaction at the cathodic region.

Key Idea

The anodic region is where metal dissolution occurs, while the cathodic region supports the reduction reaction.

When two different metals are electrically connected in the presence of an electrolyte, an electrochemical cell can form.

The more active metal can behave as the anodic material and undergo corrosion, while the more noble metal behaves as the cathodic material.

Engineering Significance

Contact between dissimilar metals can therefore influence the corrosion behaviour of a structure.

Differential aeration corrosion occurs when different areas of the same metal surface are exposed to different concentrations of oxygen.

The region with lower oxygen concentration can become anodic relative to the region with higher oxygen concentration.

Remember

Difference in oxygen concentration can create anodic and cathodic regions on the same metal.

  • Nature of metal: Different metals have different tendencies to undergo corrosion.
  • Environment: The surrounding medium can influence corrosion.
  • Temperature: Changes in temperature can affect reaction rates.
  • Moisture: Presence of water can support electrochemical corrosion processes.
  • Oxygen concentration: Differences in oxygen availability can create differential aeration conditions.

Corrosion can be controlled by reducing the interaction between the metal and its environment or by modifying the electrochemical conditions.

Protective Coatings

Create a barrier between the metal and corrosive environment.

Material Selection

Selecting suitable materials can reduce corrosion problems.

Cathodic Protection

Controls the electrochemical behaviour of the metal.

Environmental Control

Controlling environmental conditions can reduce corrosion.

Corrosion Penetration Rate (CPR) represents the rate at which a metal loses thickness due to corrosion.

CPR = K × W / (D × A × T)
Engineering Meaning

CPR allows engineers to express corrosion damage as a rate of material thickness loss over time.

CPR

Corrosion penetration rate

K

Constant determined by the selected CPR unit

W

Weight loss of the metal

D

Density of the metal

A

Exposed surface area

T

Exposure time

Weight Loss — W

Greater mass loss indicates more material has been removed from the metal during exposure.

Density — D

Density connects the measured mass loss with the corresponding volume of metal lost.

Area — A

The same amount of material loss distributed over a larger area corresponds to a different penetration rate.

Time — T

CPR expresses material loss as a rate, so the exposure duration is an important part of the calculation.

Parameter Standard Unit
Weight (W) mg
Density (D) g/cm³
Area (A) cm² / in²
Time (T) hours
Important

The required standard area unit depends on the selected CPR output unit.

For CPR CPR = K × W / (D × A × T)
For W W = CPR × D × A × T / K
For D D = K × W / (CPR × A × T)
For A A = K × W / (CPR × D × T)
For T T = K × W / (CPR × D × A)

CPR is useful for comparing the rate of metal thickness loss under different corrosion conditions.

Think Like an Engineer

A CPR value is not just a number. It represents a rate of material loss and can be used when considering the service life and corrosion behaviour of metallic components.

  • Corrosion is the deterioration of metal due to chemical or electrochemical interaction with the environment.
  • Electrochemical corrosion involves anodic and cathodic reactions.
  • Metal dissolution occurs at the anodic region.
  • Differential aeration results from differences in oxygen concentration.
  • CPR represents the rate of metal thickness loss.
  • Always use compatible units before applying the CPR equation.
  • CPR can be rearranged to calculate W, D, A or T when the other variables are known.
